01 · The category

Path tools over-claim. That is the product, not a bug.

Multi-touch and “true ROAS” products need a continuous person across ads, site, and checkout. After ITP, ATT, and link-tracking protections, that chain is broken for a large share of traffic — especially Safari and iOS.

When the graph is sparse, models still output neat credit. Founders see stacked revenue that does not reconcile to the till. The category problem is not “bad vendors.” It is selling path certainty on rails privacy removed.

Path credit trains the model. Cash MER trains the Monday budget.

02 · Safari ITP

Cookies that used to last months now last days — or hours.

Intelligent Tracking Prevention caps client-side cookies set by JavaScript. In practice that means short windows (on the order of a week for many JS cookies), and much shorter after certain click-ID landings — often measured in about a day before storage is purged.

If your “attribution” depends on a browser cookie sticking around long enough to stitch a later purchase, Safari quietly ends the experiment. Desktop Chrome is not the whole internet.

03 · iOS LTP

Click IDs get stripped before they ever reach your stack.

Link Tracking Protection and related iOS behaviors remove or rewrite tracking parameters on links users tap in Mail, Messages, and other system surfaces. The ad platform may still report a click. Your site may never see the click ID the pixel needed.

No ID in → no trustworthy match out. Suites paper over the gap with modeled fills. Finance still only sees what cleared the till.

04 · CNAME cloaking

First-party DNS tricks hit an IP-distance wall.

For a while, vendors routed tracking through a CNAME on your domain so cookies looked “first-party.” Browsers responded with IP-distance and related checks: if the CNAME resolves far from your real origin, the cloaking collapses and the cookie advantages go away.

DNS work is not a durable moat. It is an arms race — and Apple, Safari, and peers keep tightening the rules. Complexity for your team; diminishing returns for truth.

05 · CAPI & bots

Server events can poison the same loop they claim to fix.

Conversions API was meant to replace the brittle browser pixel. But a server event accepts whatever your backend — or a bot — claims happened. Invalid and automated traffic is a well-documented industry problem; credible estimates land somewhere in the mid-twenties to low-thirties percent range depending on vertical and defenses. We cite that as context, not as a measurement of your store.

The takeaway is simpler than the number: if junk events enter CAPI, delivery optimizes toward noise. More server events is not more truth.

06 · How the suites respond

Same broken rails. Different packaging.

We are not alleging fraud. We are naming approaches founders already pay for — and what those approaches still depend on.

Triple Whale–class

Operator OS plus a “pixel” that, under the hood, is still JavaScript, UTMs, and cookies — not a new physics of causality. Useful workflows; path theater stays on privacy-era rails.

Northbeam–class

Attribution court with heavier DNS and install friction, often behind a high price wall. More ceremony around the same identity problem browsers already constrained.

Polar–class

Warehouse / BI depth for teams that want pipelines and latency. Not a Monday cash desk. Still typically sits on pixel or event identity assumptions for “who drove.”

GMV-scaled suite pricing taxes growth while the underlying graph stays incomplete. Mcfly’s bet is the opposite: a flat desk fee and a number finance will accept.
